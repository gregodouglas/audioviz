**Black hole (2026-10-17, evening).** The lensed starfield became a real black hole, Interstellar-style: `starfield.wgsl` marches rays through the Schwarzschild null-geodesic field (midpoint steps, up to 120), intersecting a thin equatorial disk between the ISCO and 9.5 rs; pixels whose impact parameter clears the disk take the second-order weak-field deflection analytically, and marched rays get the weak-field residual on exit so the two paths meet without a seam. The far side of the disk folds over and under the shadow; Doppler beaming (shift^3) and gravitational redshift make the approaching (left) side brighter and bluer; the disk texture is Keplerian-sheared periodic value noise in (azimuth, log r). Stars are hash-grid points sized in *pixel* space through the inverse Jacobian of the sky lookup (`dpdx`/`dpdy` of the octahedral map), so they stay sub-pixel under magnification; bright ones get four-point diffraction and a halo, and the field fades where a pixel spans more than a cell (photon-ring smear). Audio: rs breathes with the slewed bass, disk brightness and turbulence follow energy / onsets, the beat pulses the innermost orbits, stars sparkle on transients and breathe on a per-star band. With the default tint the disk runs blackbody (red → amber → white); any other tint drives a tint→white ramp. `StarfieldParams` keeps its name (mass, density, twinkle, drift) and gains disk, doppler, tilt; `grid` is gone. Measured 190 fps uncapped at 1440x2560 fullscreen with ribbons and scope layered on top (debug build).

**Layer levelling + reorder (2026-10-17, from Greg's first look at layers).** Greg's ribbons layer (intensity 0.05, 950 particles) vanished under a scope layer whatever the scope's opacity: the composite is additive and auto exposure levels the *sum*, so a layer thousands of times dimmer than its neighbour can never win. Now, with auto exposure on, each layer is levelled before the mix: `layer_down.wgsl` box-downsamples the layer by 8 (16 bilinear taps, full coverage so thin traces count), `layer_meter.wgsl` keeps a 1x1 ping-ponged gain per layer that aims the mean over the *lit* blocks at 0.06 (a frame mean would never reach sparse dots), slewed over 1.5 s and clamped to 0.05..10000; `layer.wgsl` multiplies opacity by that gain (flag bit 0). Opacity is now the mix ratio. Layer rows in the panel have a drag handle; dropping on another row moves the layer (settings, live visual state, band filter and meter travel together; the edited index follows).

**Per-layer tint (2026-10-17, same round).** Greg wanted one layer on the album art and another on its own colour. `Layer.tint: LayerTint { mode: scene | art | custom, color }` (default scene, so old files are unchanged); the director resolves it per layer each frame (scene = the smoothed scene tint, art = the art's dominant colour or the scene tint when there is no art, custom = the layer's colour) and slews it at the same 0.8 s as the scene tint; it travels with a reorder. The panel's layer section has a Tint row (scene / album art / own colour + picker and presets); the old "Tint (all layers)" block is now "Scene tint".

**Title overlay gone; H / Tab fixed (2026-10-17, same round).** The Swiss-grid title overlay (`overlay.rs`, the `T` key, `type_overlay` / `type_scale`) is removed: what is playing lives on the strip only. The show/hide flakiness had three causes, all found by posting real key messages to the window from a script and screenshotting after each: (1) egui-winit reports **Tab as consumed unconditionally** (focus traversal), so the Tab toggle never reached the app; (2) egui's `wants_keyboard_input` is true whenever *any* widget has focus, e.g. a clicked button, so after any panel interaction H and Tab were swallowed. H also restores what it hid (the panel if it was open, and the bar/strip for one auto-hide period) instead of only clearing the flag.

**Kaleidoscope (2026-10-17, evening).** Two pieces. (1) A per-layer **fold** (`Layer.fold`, `fold.wgsl`): after a layer renders, one pass mirrors its output before the mix, radial (two mirrors, N segments) or tiled (three mirrors at 60°, an equilateral-triangle IFS fold of the plane), four rotated-grid taps for the seams, mirror-repeat sampling; spin follows energy and onsets, zoom breathes with the bass, all scaled by `react`. Works on every visual (a folded black hole is a six-point star). (2) A **Kaleidoscope visual** (`VisualKind::Kaleido`, `scenes/kaleido.rs`, `kaleido.wgsl`): a drifting, turning, aspect-correct window into a picture, two slots with a 1.5 s crossfade; the picture is the album art (re-uploaded when the track id changes) or **your own photos** from `Settings.photos_folder` (jpg/png, shuffled, decoded on a background thread at ≤1536 px, one request per dwell). Picking the visual turns the layer's fold on; with the fold off it is a music-driven slideshow. Built-in scene "Kaleidoscope". Auto exposure is now coverage-aware (`expo.wgsl`): the target rises from the dark-field 0.03 toward 0.15 as the frame fills, so a photo reads as a photo while traces and disks are untouched. Note: `target` is a reserved word in WGSL.

**Modifier chain (2026-10-17, late).** Greg: "this unlocks modifiers in my head." The fold became the first of a per-layer **ordered chain of modifiers** (`Layer.modifiers: Vec<Modifier>`, up to 6; serde-tagged enum `kind`): each is one full-screen pass over the layer's picture between the visual and the mix. The director ping-pongs two textures per layer, keeps a history pair for feedback, and advances per-slot state (angles, beat counters, ripple rings) before issuing the passes. One generic 80-byte `ModParams` uniform (p0..p3) serves every shader. Kinds: **kaleidoscope** (radial / pinwheel / tiled mirrors, segments, spin, zoom, *apex offset + wander* which is what makes the patterns rich, step-on-beats segment changes), **feedback** (echo: last frame zoomed, spun, faded under this one; bounded), **polar** (wrap / unwrap), **ripple** (rings on beats + standing sine), **edge** (Sobel, own hue or tint, keep-original mix), **chroma split** (radial or sideways, pulses with treble/onsets), **mask** (circle / band / hex, soft, invert: lets layers share the frame by place). Every kind has a `react` amount. Panel: modifier rows with switch, drag handle (own payload type), remove; add row; the selected modifier's controls. The one-build `fold` field migrates into the chain on load (`Layer::normalize`, `LegacyFold`, never written back). `Layer` is no longer `Copy`. Visual / Tint rows now wrap (four visuals). Then, same night, two more kinds: **fractal** (`fractal.wgsl`: kaleidoscopic IFS, fold / rotate / scale / shift per level with the picture read at every level, and a Julia mode that iterates z²+c with c orbiting and onsets nudging it; twist and scale breathe with the music) and **luminescence** (`lumi.wgsl`, two render targets via `scene::pipeline2` / `fullscreen_pass2`: a dual-phosphor model with a fast pool in the layer tint and a slow hue-shifted afterglow pool kept in the layer's history pair, fluorescent saturation of excited areas, and soft sparkle points on transients where the slow pool is charged). Both are plain chain entries. Per layer the history pair is shared by feedback and luminescence.

**Colour plumbing audit (2026-10-17, late).** Greg: casting stays experimental; "make sure our visualizations are plumbed for our colors… more neon when I have neon palette, especially with fluorescence on, a 'Laser Tag' feel." Findings, with neon + luminescence on ribbons: (1) ribbons took each particle's palette colour at its pitch-class position across the *whole* palette, so a chord was rainbow confetti rather than palette-led; (2) luminescence glowed in the layer *tint* (green, afterglow hue-shifted to purple) regardless of palette or picture, which turned everything grey-mauve; (3) AgX rolls saturated bright colour to white, the opposite of a neon tube; (4) the scope ignored the palette. Fixes: ribbons **Colour spread** (`RibbonsParams.spread`, default 0.3): the chord sits around the palette's moving position, each class offset by its circle-of-fifths distance from the key times the spread; luminescence **Glow in the picture's colours** (`own_colour`, default on): the pool state now remembers the exciting pixel's chroma (r/y − 1, b/y − 1 in the state's zw), the fresh glow is that colour and the afterglow is it turned by the hue shift in YIQ, so a palette carries straight through (tint mode kept as the toggle's off state); Picture gains **Look** (`PostSettings.look`: film = AgX, neon = the AgX curve applied to the brightest channel only, hue and saturation kept, cores whiten only above ~6× white) and **Saturation** (before the tonemapper; `PostParams` is 48 bytes now); the **scope** takes the palette (key class sets the base position, beam speed moves along it by 0.35; `ScopeParams` grew the table, 592 bytes; the prelude helper takes the params as an argument because the prelude is prepended to every shader). **Layer blend modes (2026-10-18).** Greg: reordering layers changed nothing. True: the composite was a hardware-additive sum, which is order-free. Now each layer has `Layer.blend` (add, over, screen, multiply; default add, so old files look the same) and the compositor is a shader pass (`layer_blend.wgsl`) that reads the composite so far from a second texture: layers go **bottom to top so slot 1 ends on top**, the composite ping-pongs between `composite` and `composite_b` with the parity chosen so the last pass lands in `composite`, and the first pass meets black (flag bit 1). Over uses the layer's brightness as coverage (`c + d·(1 − max channel)`), screen is `d + c·(1 − d)` clamped so HDR stays sane, multiply masks with the raw picture (`d · mix(1, raw·level, opacity)`). The additive pipeline is gone; `LayerParams._pad` became `blend`. Blend dropdown on the Visual tab; the Layers hint says 1 is on top.

**Director fades layers (2026-10-18, evening).** First item of the roadmap's "Next" list. The director no longer freezes the composite and dissolves it; each slot has an eased opacity ramp (`director::Fade`: from, to, smoothstep progress, seconds, delay) aimed at the slot's target every frame (its opacity when on and not soloed out, else 0). A scene switch calls `begin_fade((seconds, stagger))`: slots whose target changes ramp over that time; the auto director (`FADE_AUTO` = 4 s, 0.8 s stagger) brings the slots in bottom first, slot 1 last; manual switches (`FADE_MANUAL` = 0.5 s) all at once. Switching a layer on or off takes the manual time; dragging opacity follows in 80 ms. A slot whose **visual kind changes** keeps the old instance rendering in an `Outgoing` seat (its own meter, chain, palette, filter and the frozen `Layer` settings from the previous frame) directly under the new one while it fades out, then drops it; a slot that keeps its visual retunes in place and keeps its particles and persistence. This also makes picking a different visual in the dropdown a crossfade. Director state per slot is now a `Slot` struct (filter, meter, chain, palette, tint, uniform, fade) and shared GPU resources a `Pipes` struct, so one `render_layer` function serves live and outgoing layers. Verified with a scratch key-posting script: Space from Scope to Ribbons shows the scope under the ribbons at 0.2 s and 0.4 s, gone at 1.5 s. Panel hint reads "fading" instead of "dissolving". Tests for the ramp.

Method: `RUST_LOG=audioviz=debug timeout 14 target/release/audioviz.exe` with `AUDIOVIZ_DEFAULTS=1` and an `AUDIOVIZ_SETTINGS` layers JSON, fps cap 240, reading the once-a-second debug line. Per pass at 3.7 Mpx that is 0.1 ms (feedback) to 0.5 ms (edge, nine taps) for every kind but one: **fractal costs about 2.4 ms per pass**, ten times the rest. Not the arithmetic: each KIFS level reads the source at coordinates that diverge by the level scale (1.9x per step), so the deeper samples are incoherent reads across a 29 MB texture. A mip chain on the chain textures (sample level i at lod i·log2(scale)) would make them cache-friendly and also stop the deep levels aliasing; that needs mips on the modifier ping-pong textures and a downsample pass, so it is logged for later rather than done tonight. One fractal in a scene is 10% of a 60 Hz frame; six on every layer is the pathological case. Debug and release are within 5% of each other (dev profile: opt-level 1 with dependencies at 3): the cost is on the GPU. **Fractal mip pyramid (2026-10-18, late).** The follow-up from the performance pass. A fractal slot now owns a six-level mip pyramid of its input (`scene::hdr_pyramid`, `Chain.pyramid`, on demand like history): before the fractal pass the input is copied to level 0 and halved level by level with `blit.wgsl` (one linear tap at half size is a 2x2 box filter; it uses the modifier bind layout so the chain's groups serve it). The modifier's mirror sampler filters between mips. `fractal.wgsl` tracks the local magnification through the recursion (KIFS: times the level scale each step; Julia: times |2z|) and reads each level at `base_lod + log2(magnification)`, where `base_lod` accounts for the zoom. Result at 2560x1440, release: six fractals on each of three layers 20 → 62 fps; the heavy set (six mixed modifiers per layer) 63 → 98 fps. Look: the deep levels are smooth instead of sparkling, the tiling of the trace reads the same.

**Waves visual (2026-10-18, late; first of the new visuals round).** `VisualKind::Waves`, `scenes/waves.rs`, `waves.wgsl` + `waves_params.wgsl` (prelude). Palette by age (front to back) or by the row's loudness (a second storage buffer of per-row RMS); without a palette, the tint. Params: source, rows, rate, height, spread, perspective, horizon, near edge, width, gain (times the layer auto gain), smoothing, distance fade, palette by. **Halftone modifier (2026-10-18, late).** Greg: the halftone in Uberviz is "pretty cool", with "a variability of colour and monotone throughout" he reads as an animation style. `Modifier::Halftone` (`halftone.wgsl`, pipeline index 10): pixel space is rotated by the screen angle and cut into cells; each cell samples the picture at its centre (a 2x2 box so thin lines register), maps the luminance through a soft knee and a gamma into a mark size, and draws a dot (radius by the square root of the brightness, so ink area is linear), a hatch line or a cross with an anti-aliased edge. Ink: the picture's own hue at unit brightness, or one ink (the tint, or the palette by brightness), mixed by `mono`; `mono` wanders on two slow sines scaled by `drift` and jumps toward one ink on onsets, so the print flickers between colour and monotone the way Greg described. Cells breathe with the bass. Params: marks, cell, angle, gamma, one ink, drift, ink from the palette, invert, reacts; GradeParams uniform (palette shift in p2.x). Snapshot: the Sigil through dots in the neon palette. Credit recorded in `docs/INSPIRATION.md` (licence pending the research agent's findings; nothing was copied, the shader is written from the description of the effect).

**Warp modifier (2026-10-18, late).** `Modifier::Warp` (`warp.wgsl`, index 11): the picture is sampled through a flowing field of two-octave value noise (two independent fields make a vector, a third turns it by `twist`), amount in pixels swelling with the bass, the field's clock running with energy and kicked by onsets. First snapshot showed vertical tears in the field: the lattice hash took float corners, and the compiler's fused multiply-add rounds `(i + 1) * k` differently from the neighbour's `i * k`, so one corner hashed two ways. The noise now hashes integer lattice coordinates (`ihash`, a u32 mix), which no rounding can split. The prelude's `hash21` stays for the shaders that use it per pixel rather than per lattice corner. Params: amount, scale, speed, twist, detail (octaves), reacts.

**Crystal visual (2026-10-18, late; the one Greg named from Uberviz).** `VisualKind::Crystal`, `scenes/crystal.rs`, `crystal.wgsl` + `crystal_params.wgsl` (prelude). Written fresh from the research description, nothing copied (Uberviz has no licence; credit in `docs/INSPIRATION.md`). Up to sixteen tetrahedral shards (five by default, stretched 2x along their own axis) sit in a cluster that tumbles on x and z; the cluster's scale follows smoothed loudness (the mean of the three attack-normalised bands) with an onset kick. Params: count, size, stretch, tumble, scatter, new pose every N beats, facets, edges, edge width, reacts. Built-in scene "Crystal" (neon palette, bloom 0.18, neon look). ## Why

Greg's verdict on the 0.1.0 build: the scenes look considered, the shell around them does not. The stock Windows title bar, the egui debug window, and the 2005-era Inno wizard all read as "developer tool". The product should feel like one object: the window, the panel, the installer and the mark share one design language.

## Design language (fixed before any code)

- **Reference:** VS Code's integrated title bar for the chrome; Linear / Raycast for the panel density; the Swiss-grid title overlay already in the starfield scene for type.
- **Surface:** near-black `#0b0e10` with a 1 px hairline `#1c2226`; no gradients, no drop shadows except the DWM window shadow.
- **Type:** the overlay's grotesk (Segoe UI → Helvetica Neue fallback chain in `overlay.rs`), 12/13/15/22 px scale; numbers in a tabular monospace (Cascadia Mono / SF Mono fallback).
- **Accent:** the phosphor tint from settings (`settings.tint`, default `#66ff8c`) used only for state: active scene pip, focused control, calibration pulse. Everything else is grey.
- **Motion:** 120 ms ease-out for show/hide; nothing bounces.
- **Density:** 28 px chrome bar, 32 px control rows, 8 px grid.

## What the second pass adds (the UX, by state)

**Who it is for:** someone with Spotify open and a second monitor, running this for hours and touching it a few times a night. Every addition below is judged by "does it earn its place when the user is not looking at it?"

Ambient (most of the time)
- Nothing on screen but the scene. Chrome, strip and notices are all hover-revealed and fade after 3 s of mouse rest; fullscreen hides them too.
- Track-change toast: art thumb, title, artist, bottom-left, 4 s, in the scenes without a title overlay. Off switch in Display.
- **Reduce flashing** toggle (Display): caps the frame-to-frame luminance delta for photosensitive viewers. This is a safety feature, on by default for the first run on an unknown display, and it ships before anything cosmetic.

Hover (bar + strip)
- Bar: mark · name · scene pips (1 2 3, A auto) · status chips (capture source, sync ms, monitor + fps) · window controls. Chips are read-only glances; clicking one opens the matching panel section.
- Strip: album art, title, artist · album, previous / play-pause / next via the Windows media session (SMTC supports control, not just reading), progress with elapsed / total. Controlling Spotify from the visualizer on the second monitor is the single most useful addition.
- **Tint from album art**: the scene accent follows the dominant art colour (we already receive the art). Toggle on the strip; the fixed phosphor tint remains the default.
- `?` shows every shortcut as an overlay.

Panel
- Docked right, translucent over the scene, icon rail: Scene, Post, Sync, Display, Looks, Meters, About.
- **Looks**: named presets of all settings (Night, Party, Studio ship as examples). Save current, export / import JSON (share a look with someone). Scene-and-look are what a non-tinkerer actually wants to switch.
- Display: monitor picker as a small diagram of the monitors (like Windows display settings), start fullscreen on it, fps cap, low-power mode (30 fps when the window is not visible or on battery), toast on/off, reduce flashing.
- About: version, license, privacy summary, **Copy diagnostics** (GPU, backend, capture mode, fps, settings path) for bug reports, link to the releases page (opened in the browser, the app itself never goes online).
- Meters behind a developer toggle.

Empty state (Spotify not running or nothing playing)
- One sentence, which device is being listened to meanwhile, two actions: Open Spotify, Pick another app (sets the capture target). Replaces the current bottom-left notice for this case.

Calibration
- Full overlay: pulsing tap ring, dots per tap (matched / missed), the numbers, and a verdict ("Looking good" under ±15 ms, "Taps are scattered, try a track with a clear kick" otherwise). Enter applies, C cancels.

Tray
- Start-at-sign-in installs minimised to the tray. Tray menu: show, scene, fullscreen on monitor N, pause capture, quit. For an ambient app the tray is the primary control surface when the window is on the other monitor.

Installer (Inno, step 1)
- Checks before installing: Windows build, DirectX 12 GPU name, Spotify present, current output device. Failing a check explains it on the page instead of failing after install.
- Upgrade banner with "your settings are kept"; location with Change; three options (Start Menu, desktop, start at sign-in minimised to tray); one footer line with size, the unsigned status and where the checksum is.
- Finish page: what was done, Launch, Open Spotify too, Start fullscreen on my second monitor, one tip.
- Uninstaller: same visual, "keep my settings" default, done in one page.

Not in this phase: album-art-driven scenes beyond the tint, multiple windows, macOS.

## Scope, in build order

1. **Chrome** (#1). `with_decorations(false)`; an egui top bar: mark · "audioviz" · scene pips (1 2 3, active in accent) · now-playing (artist — title, truncated) · min / max / close. Drag to move via `window.drag_window()`, edges via `drag_resize_window()`. Snap layouts and Aero Snap need `WM_NCHITTEST`: subclass the HWND with the `windows` crate (`SetWindowSubclass`) and return `HTCAPTION` for the bar, `HTLEFT`… for an 6 px border, `HTCLIENT` elsewhere; keep DWM rounded corners with `DwmSetWindowAttribute(DWMWA_WINDOW_CORNER_PREFERENCE)`. Bar hides in fullscreen and in windowed mode after 3 s without mouse movement; reappears on movement near the top edge. macOS: `with_titlebar_transparent` + `with_fullsize_content_view` later (Phase 7).
2. **Panel** (#2). Replace `egui::Window` with a right-docked `SidePanel` (320 px, slides in): icon rail on its left edge (Scene, Post, Sync, Display, Meters, About), one section at a time, section title + reset at the top, sliders with tabular readouts, keyboard hints in the footer. Custom `egui::Style`: no window frames, 4 px radii, hairline separators, accent on focus only. Meters (Beat, Level, Chroma) move behind a "developer" toggle in Display. Panel state (open section, developer) persists in settings.
3. **Installer** (#3), step 1: Inno `WizardStyle=modern`, `WizardSizePercent=120`, custom `WizardImageFile`/`WizardSmallImageFile` rendered by `cargo xtask icon` (mark on `#0b0e10`), `DisableWelcomePage=no` with a single sentence, `DisableReadyPage=yes`, finish page with "Launch" only. Uninstaller gets the same images. Step 2 (bespoke Rust installer or MSIX) stays in `docs/ROADMAP.md` until signing exists.
4. **First run** (#4). Three-step sheet in the new panel style: monitor, tap calibration, starting scene. Skippable; never shown again; each step reachable from the panel.
5. **Brand** (#5). Stroke weights at 16/24/32 px, monochrome variant for the bar, wizard images, all from `assets/icon.svg` via xtask.

Out of scope: new scenes, analysis changes, macOS.

## Risks

- winit 0.30 on Windows with `decorations(false)` loses the DWM shadow and snap unless the hit-test hook is right; budget a day for the `WM_NCHITTEST` subclass and test with Win+arrow, Snap Layouts hover on the max button, and the 4K/240 Hz plus portrait monitor pair.
- egui `SidePanel` steals the full window height; the scene must render underneath it (translucent panel over the composite) or the composite viewport shrinks. Decision: translucent (`#0b0e10` at 92 %), scene unchanged.
- Any change to `Settings` must migrate (see `config::migrate_post` for the pattern); never drop a tuned value.
